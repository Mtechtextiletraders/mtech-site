Getting the right part

How to match a spinning spare part

A part that almost fits costs more than it saves. This is how to make sure it is the right one.

01

Machine first, then the part

Tell us the make and model of the machine first, then the part. The same kind of part, a top roller or a spindle for example, comes in different sizes and designs for different machines, and sometimes for different versions of the same model.

02

Part number, photos, measurements

The surest match is the part number from the machine's parts catalogue or printed on the old part. If there is no number, send clear photos of the old part from more than one side, ideally next to a measuring tape, with its key sizes: diameter, length, bore, and the number of teeth on a gear.

03

New or pre-owned

We supply both. New parts make sense for wear items that decide yarn quality, such as cots, aprons, travellers and card wire. Good pre-owned parts can be a practical choice for slow-wearing parts, especially on older machines where new ones are hard to find. Ask about the condition before you decide.

04

Know which parts wear

  • Ring frame: rings and travellers, cots, aprons and spindle tapes affect evenness and hairiness, so mills replace them on a schedule.
  • Carding: cylinder, doffer and licker-in wire and flat tops lose sharpness over time, and blunt wire shows up as neps.
  • Winding: splicer parts and drums on an Autoconer affect splice strength and cone build.
05

Test one before you order many

When the same part is going onto many spindles or deliveries, fit and run one first. It is the easiest way to confirm size and finish before the full quantity is ordered.

06

Stock and delivery

Some parts are in stock and others are arranged on order. Parts are delivered all over Pakistan. If a part is not on our stock page, ask anyway: we check the godown and our network.

07

Keep a record of what you fit

A simple list of part numbers, sources and fitting dates for each machine makes the next order faster, and shows which parts are wearing out sooner than they should. That is often the first sign of a setting or alignment problem.

08

Older machines, hard-to-find parts

For older or less common models, some new parts are no longer made. Good pre-owned parts taken from dismantled machines are often the practical answer, which is one reason our spare parts and machinery work go hand in hand.

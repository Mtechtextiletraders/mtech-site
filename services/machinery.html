How cotton becomes yarn

From the first bale to the final cone.

The departments of a spinning mill, in order, and the makes we most often buy and sell for each.

  1. 01

    Blowroom

    Bales → Clean tufts

    Compressed bales are opened, the cotton is plucked into small tufts, cleaned of trash and blended so every kilo runs the same.

    Trützschler · Rieter blowroom lines

  2. 02

    Carding

    Tufts → Card sliver

    The card separates the fibres one by one into a fine web, then gathers that web into a soft rope called sliver.

    Trützschler TC · Rieter C series

  3. 03

    Draw frame

    6 to 8 slivers → One even sliver

    Several slivers are doubled and drafted together. Thick and thin places cancel out and the fibres lie straight.

    Rieter RSB · Trützschler TD

  4. 04

    Comber

    Sliver → Combed sliver

    Short fibres and neps are combed out. What is left makes finer, stronger and cleaner yarn.

    Rieter E series · Marzoli

  5. 05

    Simplex

    Sliver → Roving

    Sliver is drawn thinner and given a light twist so it can be wound onto bobbins for the ring frame.

    Toyota FL · Zinser · Marzoli

  6. 06

    Ring frame

    Roving → Yarn

    The final draft and twist. Thousands of spindles turn roving into yarn, wound onto small cops.

    Zinser · Toyota RX · Marzoli · LMW

  7. 07

    Autoconer

    Cops → Cones

    Yarn from the cops is checked by the clearer, faults are cut and spliced, and it is wound onto large cones ready to sell.

    Murata · Schlafhorst · Savio

  8. 08

    Open-end

    Sliver → Yarn

    Rotor spinning skips the simplex and ring frame and makes coarser yarn straight from sliver, at high speed.

    Rieter R · Schlafhorst Autocoro

Buying guide

Buying or selling used spinning machinery

What to look at before you agree a price, whether you are buying one machine or a whole section.

01

Machines we deal in

Our everyday work is with spinning machinery: Autocoro, RN and other open-end (rotor) machines, ring frames including Zinser, Autoconer winding machines, blowroom lines and draw frames — new and pre-owned, plus machinery we import for clients. Send the make, model and year of what you need or want to sell.

02

Start with make, model and year

The make, model and year of manufacture tell you which generation of technology you are getting, what speeds and counts the machine was built for, and how easy its parts will be to find. Two ring frames with the same spindle count can differ a lot in drafting system, drive and automation, so always note the serial number plate as well as the model name.

03

See it running if you can

A machine still running in a mill tells you more than one standing in a shed. Whether that is possible depends on the machine and where it is. If it is not running, look closely at the parts that wear: spindles and drafting rollers on a ring frame, wire on a card, splicers and drums on an Autoconer, rotors and opening rollers on open-end.

04

Check what comes with it

Ask exactly what is included: motors and drives, electrical panels, control units, suction and pneumafil, cans, bobbins and any spare parts. Missing accessories add cost and delay later, so they belong in the price discussion, not after it.

05

Match it to your line

A machine has to fit the sections before and after it. The output of blowroom, carding and draw frames should balance the ring frames or rotors they feed, and the counts you plan to spin decide whether you need combers, compact spinning or open-end. If you are unsure, ask before you buy.

06

Plan dismantling, transport and installation

Used machinery has to be dismantled, marked, loaded, moved and set up again. Agree who does each step, and at whose cost, before the deal is closed. M Tech arranges installation and shifting on request; it is charged separately from the machine.

07

Selling? Prepare the details

For a quick and fair valuation, send the make, model, year, number of spindles, deliveries or heads, the present condition, and clear photos of the nameplate and the main parts. The more complete the details, the more useful the first answer. Machinery we have right now is on our stock page, which changes every week.
